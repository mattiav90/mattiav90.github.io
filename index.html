About

I design asynchronous (quasi-delay-insensitive) VLSI circuits in the Asynchronous VLSI and Architecture Lab at Yale, advised by Prof. Rajit Manohar. My thesis builds the first fully asynchronous low-power, low-latency ASIC for convolutional neural network inference — an edge-AI architecture that computes in integer-only quantized arithmetic and exploits highly unstructured sparsity in both weights and activations.

Much of the work is hardware–software co-design: the pruning scheme, the quantization, and the dataflow are chosen together, and the design is mapped into both synchronous and asynchronous circuit families to measure the energy and delay trade-off directly.

Before VLSI I worked on integrated photonics in Prof. Hong Tang's lab, contributing to the first photonic-integrated-circuit titanium:sapphire laser, and spent two years as a software engineer building industrial machine-vision systems in Italy.

Current projects

SPACE Accelerator

An asynchronous, handshake-based CNN inference accelerator written in ACT. A tile-based stationary-weight PE array that skips zero weights and zero activations, runs int8 quantized convolutions, and is verified instruction-by-instruction against a Python golden model and ONNX Runtime. Current work: filling the array, cutting halo waste, and co-designing the pruning scheme around the hardware's real cost model.

Asynchronous RISC-V tapeout Silicon

Contributing to the tapeout of a fully asynchronous RISC-V chip.
